What Is Batch Wise Inventory Management?

Batch wise inventory management means tracking products separately according to their batch or lot number.

Instead of treating all units of the same product as one stock quantity, each batch is maintained with important information such as:

  • Product name
  • Batch number
  • Manufacturing date
  • Expiry date
  • Purchase date
  • Purchase price
  • Selling price or MRP
  • Quantity received
  • Quantity sold
  • Available stock
  • Supplier details

For example, suppose a pharmacy has 100 units of a medicine.

The stock may actually consist of:

Batch Manufacturing Date Expiry Date Quantity
B101January 2026December 202640
B205March 2026February 202735
B318May 2026April 202725

Although all 100 units are the same medicine, they should not necessarily be treated as one undifferentiated stock quantity.

The first batch expires earlier, so it should generally be sold before the later batches.

This is where batch wise inventory management becomes important.

Why Is Batch Tracking Important for Expiry-Based Products?

Businesses dealing with expiry-based products cannot rely only on total stock quantities.

Knowing that you have 500 units available does not tell you:

  • Which batch the units belong to
  • Which batch expires first
  • How much stock is nearing expiry
  • Which products have already expired
  • Which batch should be sold first
  • How much stock is sitting unsold for too long

Batch tracking solves this problem by giving businesses visibility into stock at the batch level.

1. Helps Prevent Expired Stock

When expiry dates are recorded against individual batches, businesses can identify products that are approaching their expiry dates.

This gives the business time to take action instead of discovering expired products during a physical stock check.

2. Reduces Inventory Wastage

Expired products generally cannot be sold.

If a business allows products to expire on the shelf, the purchase cost of that inventory can effectively become a loss.

Proper batch tracking helps businesses identify slow-moving and near-expiry products earlier.

3. Improves Stock Rotation

Businesses can prioritize the sale of batches that are closer to expiry.

This helps maintain healthier inventory movement and reduces the chances of older stock being left behind.

4. Makes Stock Audits Easier

Instead of manually checking every product and searching through purchase records, businesses can use batch-level information to understand where their inventory stands.

5. Improves Traceability

If there is an issue with a particular batch, businesses can identify the affected stock more easily.

This is particularly useful for pharmacies, manufacturers, distributors, food businesses, and other industries where batch traceability matters.

FIFO vs FEFO: Which Method Is Better for Expiry-Based Inventory?

Two common inventory rotation methods are FIFO and FEFO.

FIFO: First In, First Out

FIFO means the products that entered the inventory first should be sold first.

For example:

  • Batch A purchased in January
  • Batch B purchased in March

Under FIFO, Batch A would generally be sold before Batch B.

FIFO works well when older inventory is also more likely to expire first.

However, purchase date and expiry date are not always directly related.

FEFO: First Expiry, First Out

FEFO means:

First Expiry, First Out.

Under FEFO, the batch with the earliest expiry date is prioritized for sale, regardless of when it was purchased.

For expiry-based products, FEFO is often more practical than relying only on FIFO.

For example:

Batch Purchase Date Expiry Date Selling Priority
A101JanuaryOctober1
B202FebruaryDecember2
C303MarchSeptember1

Although Batch C303 was purchased last, it expires first. Therefore, it should be prioritized under FEFO.

How to Manage Batch Wise Inventory Effectively

A proper batch inventory system should connect purchasing, stock management, billing, and expiry tracking.

Here is a practical workflow.

Step 1: Create the Product

Start by creating the product in your inventory system.

Record information such as:

  • Product name
  • Category
  • Unit
  • MRP
  • Selling price
  • Tax details
  • Inventory settings

Step 2: Record Batch Details During Purchase

Whenever new stock arrives, record the relevant batch information.

This may include:

  • Batch number
  • Manufacturing date
  • Expiry date
  • Quantity
  • Purchase price
  • MRP
  • Supplier
  • Purchase date

This creates a clear inventory record from the beginning.

Step 3: Maintain Stock Separately by Batch

The system should distinguish between different batches of the same product.

For example:

Paracetamol 500 mg

  • Batch P101: 60 units
  • Batch P205: 100 units
  • Batch P309: 75 units

This gives the business a more accurate picture of its actual inventory.

Step 4: Sell the Appropriate Batch First

When creating a sale, the business can prioritize the batch that should leave the inventory first.

For expiry-based products, this generally means prioritizing the batch with the earliest expiry date.

Step 5: Monitor Near-Expiry Products

Near-expiry stock should be identified before it becomes expired stock.

Businesses can review their inventory regularly and identify products that are approaching their expiry dates.

This creates an opportunity to:

  • Prioritize sales
  • Offer appropriate discounts where applicable
  • Return eligible stock to suppliers
  • Adjust purchasing decisions
  • Reduce future wastage

Step 6: Remove or Manage Expired Stock

Expired products should be identified separately from sellable inventory.

Keeping expired stock mixed with active inventory can result in accidental sales and inaccurate stock reports.

A proper inventory process should clearly identify expired or unusable products.

How to Manage Near-Expiry Inventory

Near-expiry inventory is one of the biggest challenges for businesses selling products with limited shelf lives.

Suppose a store has 50 units of a product expiring within the next 30 days.

If this information is not visible, the business may continue purchasing the same product while the existing stock remains unsold.

A better approach is to regularly review:

Product → Batch → Available Quantity → Expiry Date → Days Remaining

Based on this information, businesses can decide what action to take.

Common actions for near-expiry stock include:

  • Prioritize the batch for sale
  • Stop purchasing additional stock unnecessarily
  • Offer discounts where commercially appropriate
  • Contact suppliers about eligible returns
  • Move stock to locations where demand is higher
  • Monitor the product more frequently

The objective is simple:

Identify the problem before the product expires.

What Happens When Expired Stock Is Not Managed?

Poor expiry management can create several problems.

Financial Loss

The business has already spent money purchasing the inventory, but the product may no longer be sellable.

Dead Stock

Expired products occupy storage space without contributing to revenue.

Inaccurate Inventory

If expired products remain included in available stock, the system may show more sellable inventory than the business actually has.

Customer Complaints

Selling expired products can seriously damage customer trust and create compliance and reputational concerns.

Poor Purchasing Decisions

If expired and active inventory are not clearly separated, businesses may misunderstand actual demand and continue purchasing products unnecessarily.

Common Mistakes in Expiry-Based Inventory Management

Even businesses that maintain inventory records can make mistakes when expiry information is not properly integrated into their stock management process.

Mistake 1: Tracking Only Total Quantity

Knowing that you have 1,000 units does not tell you how many belong to each batch or when those batches expire.

Mistake 2: Using Only Purchase Dates

Purchase date alone cannot always determine which product should be sold first.

Expiry date should be considered for expiry-based products.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Near-Expiry Stock

Waiting until products have already expired is too late.

Near-expiry stock should be identified early.

Mistake 4: Mixing Different Batches

Combining different batches into one undifferentiated stock record makes traceability and expiry management difficult.

Mistake 5: Relying Entirely on Manual Records

Manual registers and spreadsheets can become difficult to maintain as product volume increases.

Mistake 6: Continuing to Purchase Slow-Moving Products

If a product is repeatedly approaching expiry before being sold, the business should review its purchasing quantity and sales velocity.

Mistake 7: Not Reviewing Inventory Regularly

Even the best inventory system is only useful when businesses actually review their stock information and act on it.

Best Practices for Batch Wise Inventory Management

To improve batch and expiry management, businesses should follow a consistent process.

1. Record Batch Information at the Time of Purchase

Do not wait until the product is sold to record its batch details.

2. Always Capture Expiry Dates

Expiry information is particularly important for products with limited shelf lives.

3. Use FEFO Where Appropriate

Prioritize products with earlier expiry dates rather than relying exclusively on purchase dates.

4. Review Near-Expiry Stock Regularly

Set a regular process for identifying products approaching expiry.

5. Separate Expired Inventory

Expired stock should be clearly identified and managed separately from sellable inventory.

6. Monitor Slow-Moving Products

Products that remain in stock for long periods have a greater chance of becoming dead or expired stock.

7. Use Inventory Reports for Purchasing Decisions

Purchase quantities should be based on demand, stock levels, product movement, and shelf life.

8. Keep Billing and Inventory Connected

When sales and inventory records are connected, stock movement can be updated more efficiently.
